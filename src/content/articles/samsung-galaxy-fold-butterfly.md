## The Modern Story

The Samsung Galaxy Z Fold 6 (2024) costs $1,899. It has a 7.6-inch inner display that unfolds from a phone-sized outer screen. It's genuinely useful: a phone that becomes a tablet, thin enough to carry in a pocket, durable enough (Samsung claims) to fold 200,000 times.

Getting here took Samsung three very public failures:

- **February 2019**: Galaxy Fold announced at $1,980. Pre-orders open.
- **April 2019**: Review units sent to journalists. Within 48 hours, screens are breaking — on devices that had been unfolded less than 100 times.
- **April 22, 2019**: Samsung cancels the launch. Redesign begins.
- **September 2019**: Galaxy Fold re-released, 5 months late. Screen durability improved but hinge still creates a crease visible in all lighting conditions.
- **2021**: Galaxy Z Fold 3 — first "good" version. IPX8 water resistance, S-Pen support.
- **2023**: Galaxy Z Fold 5 — first version with a truly flat fold. The crease remains.

The engineering problem Samsung was solving in 2019 had actually been documented and partially solved in 1994 — by IBM.

## ROOT: Going Back to 1994

In 1994, IBM faced an identical engineering problem: how do you fit a full-size keyboard into a laptop that's small enough to carry comfortably?

The ThinkPad 701C — designed by legendary German industrial designer Richard Sapper — solved the problem with what became known as the "Butterfly" keyboard. The keyboard was split into two interlocking panels that folded inward when the laptop closed, and unfolded to a wider-than-the-chassis keyboard when opened.

The mechanism was a precision folding hinge that had to:
- Open and close thousands of times without failure
- Lock precisely in position when open
- Accommodate tolerances between two independently-moving panels
- Not crack, bind, or create visible gaps at the fold point

The ThinkPad 701C Butterfly keyboard was widely considered the most beautiful piece of industrial design IBM ever produced. It now sits in the Museum of Modern Art's permanent collection.

Samsung's Galaxy Fold designers were solving the exact same mechanical problem at a different scale — applied to a flexible display instead of a keyboard. The two hinge mechanisms share fundamental design principles.

**The chain:**
- **1994** — IBM Butterfly keyboard: folding mechanism for form-factor constraints
- **2000s** — Flexible display research: Sony, Samsung, LG begin OLED flex research
- **2013** — Samsung announces "Project Valley" — internal foldable phone research
- **2019** — Galaxy Fold launch and failure: crease, debris infiltration, display delamination
- **2021** — Z Fold 3: durable enough for daily use, officially "good"
- **2024** — Z Fold 6: waterproof, flat fold, crease reduced to near-invisible in normal use

## Did You Know

The Samsung Galaxy Fold display uses Ultra Thin Glass (UTG) — glass that is approximately 30 micrometers thick, compared to the 0.7mm glass on a standard smartphone screen. At 30μm, glass becomes flexible enough to bend repeatedly without fracturing. But at this thickness, it offers almost no scratch resistance — a fingernail can scratch it.

This is why Samsung still puts a protective plastic layer over the UTG. The plastic is what scratches, not the glass underneath. Early reviewers who peeled off the "screen protector" (which looked like every other pre-applied protective film) were actually removing a structural component of the display.

## Why It Matters Today

The Galaxy Fold's three failures weren't engineering incompetence. They were engineering honesty: Samsung shipped the Fold as soon as it was good enough to sell, and the market told them it wasn't good enough yet.

The IBM Butterfly keyboard was a mechanical tour de force — beautiful, clever, and expensive to manufacture. It was discontinued after two years because it was too complex and costly for mainstream production. Samsung's foldable has followed a similar trajectory: complex, expensive, improving rapidly as manufacturing scales.

Foldable phones in 2019 were where touchscreen phones were in 2007 — technically impressive, obviously the future, not quite there yet. The Galaxy Z Fold 6 is the smartphone equivalent of the iPhone 3GS: the version where it became genuinely good, but the first-generation enthusiasts already knew it was coming.

## FUTURE: Rollable and Wrappable

> ⚠️ Speculative — based on Samsung, LG, and TCL display research.

The next form factor evolution isn't folding — it's rolling. Rollable displays extend or retract like a scroll, rather than folding along a hinge line. This eliminates the crease problem entirely: there's no permanent fold point, so no permanent crease.

LG showed a rollable phone prototype (LG Rollable) in 2021 before canceling it alongside their smartphone division. TCL has demonstrated rollable TV concepts. Samsung's display division has filed patents for rollable phone designs since 2018.

**Prediction:** By 2027, a major manufacturer will ship a consumer rollable smartphone. The display will extend from a standard phone size to a 6.5-inch tablet in 1.5 seconds. The price will be above $1,500 at launch. It will fail commercially on version 1 and become mainstream by version 3 — following exactly the Galaxy Fold's trajectory.

The IBM Butterfly keyboard took two years and a MoMA exhibit to be appreciated as industrial design. Samsung's foldables will take a decade to be appreciated as the hinge point between the smartphone era and whatever comes next.
